# Seekable HTML carries the lessons; 3D plays cameos

Claude Code can produce almost any motion graphic that can be written as code and rendered without a screen: browser pages (HTML, CSS, SVG, canvas, WebGL) captured frame by frame, React compositions (Remotion), HTML-plus-GSAP compositions (HyperFrames), Python scenes (Manim, Blender's `bpy`), fragment shaders, Lottie and Rive files, ffmpeg filter graphs, desktop apps reached through MCP bridges, and generative video or voice called by API. For a creator making 60-second, 9:16 narrated copywriting lessons, the decisive finding is that **the HTML/CSS plus headless Chromium approach you have already tested is the same architecture the two leading agent video frameworks use**: seek each frame in headless Chrome, screenshot it, and encode with ffmpeg. You do not need a different engine. You need a seekable animation timeline (GSAP), real word timestamps (Whisper), and a decision about whether to keep your own capture script or adopt HyperFrames (Apache-2.0, free) or Remotion (free only up to 3 employees). 3D fits as an accent rather than a base layer. A three.js toon scene costs about **60–70 ms per 1080x1920 frame** on CPU when Chromium uses Mesa llvmpipe, so a minute of it renders in about 2 minutes. Blender EEVEE costs **2–5 s per frame**, or 1–3 hours per minute of video, which suits occasional hero shots only. The styles that best fit a Crimson & Aubergine copy lesson are all 2D and cheap: kinetic word captions, collage and mixed media, self-drawing underlines and arrows, inbox and feed mock-ups, bold summary cards, and a grain or halftone finishing layer. Desktop-app bridges (After Effects, Resolve) and generative video are complements, not foundations.

## One architecture powers nearly all agent-made video

Every serious code-to-video route for an agent in 2026 does the same thing underneath. It holds the animation still, sets the clock to frame *n*, captures the frame, and hands the stack of frames to ffmpeg. HyperFrames says its renderer "seeks each frame in headless Chrome and encodes the result with FFmpeg" and that "the same input produces the same video" ([HyperFrames](https://github.com/heygen-com/hyperframes)). Remotion renders frame by frame from `useCurrentFrame()` through Chrome Headless Shell ([Meldron09 research doc](https://github.com/Meldron09/Understand-Anything/blob/research/programmatic-video/docs/research/programmatic-video.md)). The older alternative of real-time screen recording drops frames and drifts out of sync. Virtual-time tools such as timecut override JavaScript clocks but miss CSS animations and video elements, and were last pushed in 2023 ([timecut](https://github.com/tungs/timecut)). Your existing `render(t)` page captured with Playwright is therefore not a stopgap. It is a hand-built version of the industry pattern, and the choice now is how much framework to wrap around it.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Missing: captions tooling, block library, render parallelism |

**HyperFrames** is the newest and most agent-oriented hub. Its tagline is "Write HTML. Render video. Built for agents." It ships seven skills, a captions block, `<audio>` elements with `data-start`, `data-duration` and `data-volume` that its producer mixes, and adapters for "GSAP, CSS, Lottie, Three.js, Anime.js, WAAPI, or your own frame adapter" ([HyperFrames](https://github.com/heygen-com/hyperframes)). The npm package reached **0.8.223 on 31 October 2026** under Apache-2.0 ([npm hyperframes](https://registry.npmjs.org/hyperframes)). One team's benchmark put 10 seconds of 1080p at **25.0 s unoptimised and 9.8 s optimised**, roughly 1–2.5 minutes of rendering per minute of output. The same source notes it needs Node 22+ and system ffmpeg, turns telemetry on by default (`HYPERFRAMES_NO_TELEMETRY=1` disables it), and fails at site capture through HTTP proxies ([Meldron09 research doc](https://github.com/Meldron09/Understand-Anything/blob/research/programmatic-video/docs/research/programmatic-video.md)). That team chose HyperFrames over Remotion as its default "for explainers, mechanisms, charts, kinetic text, code walkthroughs" ([Meldron09 issue #3](https://github.com/Meldron09/Understand-Anything/issues/3)). Its weaknesses are youth (repo created March 2026, 300+ open issues) and the fact that its README shows only a 1920x1080 example. 9:16 presets and caption-highlight details are undocumented, though nothing stops a 1080x1920 page.

**Remotion** remains the most mature option. Version 4.0.539 shipped on 26 October 2026 ([npm remotion](https://registry.npmjs.org/remotion)). It announced Agent Skills on 20 January 2026 ("make videos just with Claude Code!") ([Remotion on X](https://x.com/Remotion/status/2013626968386765291)), and its skills repo covers creation, captions, rendering and multimedia ([remotion-dev/skills](https://github.com/remotion-dev/skills)). Its caption chain is the strongest available. Whisper.cpp output goes through `toCaptions()`, then `createTikTokStyleCaptions()` groups words into pages with per-word timing, and `combineTokensWithinMilliseconds` controls how many words appear at once ([Remotion docs](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/install-whisper-cpp/convert-to-captions)). An official `template-tiktok` repo packages that pipeline ([template-tiktok](https://github.com/remotion-dev/template-tiktok)). The catch is the licence. It is free for individuals and "a for-profit organization with up to 3 employees", and free use covers commercial video output ([Remotion LICENSE.md](https://github.com/remotion-dev/remotion/blob/main/LICENSE.md)). Above that, a Company Licence applies. Third parties quote **$25 per seat per month** for Creators and $0.01 per render with a $100 monthly minimum for Automators, but one snippet cites a $500 minimum, so treat prices as unverified ([Meldron09 research doc](https://github.com/Meldron09/Understand-Anything/blob/research/programmatic-video/docs/research/programmatic-video.md)). Practitioners also warn that anything not driven by `useCurrentFrame()`, such as CSS transitions or `setTimeout`, renders wrongly ([claude-code-handbook](https://github.com/ThamJiaHe/claude-code-handbook/blob/main/docs/motion-graphics-claude-remotion-guide.md)), that every `@remotion/*` package must sit on exactly the same version, and that Remotion "doesn't know what you said", so word timing needs a separate transcription step ([Rescroll](https://www.rescroll.ai/blog/claude-code-remotion-motion-graphics-2026)).

**Claude Motion** is Anthropic's own entry and is real, not rumour. The support article describes a beta that turns content into "short animations" such as explainers and animated charts. It is generated as code, "doesn't use a video generation model", is editable by chat or in an editor, and exports MP4. It is on by default for Team plans and off until an owner enables it on Enterprise ([Claude Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/17454997-get-started-with-claude-motion)). It launched around 8 October 2026 alongside Claude Dashboards ([Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/anthropic-launches-claude-dashboards-and-motion-takes-docs-out-of-beta/)). The help page states no aspect ratios, duration limits or audio support. A Canva connector tool for importing Claude Motion projects is already visible, but for exact 1080x1920 renders locked to a narration track it is not yet a replacement for a code pipeline you control. Hosted alternatives exist too. Motion by Mosaic offers a remote MCP connector that builds code-based motion graphics on Motion credits ([Mosaic docs](https://docs.mosaic.so/how-to/motion-for-claude.md)).

**Revideo and Motion Canvas** are weaker bets. Motion Canvas's last stable release was v3.17.2 in December 2024, and it exports through its editor UI with no documented headless CLI. Revideo, its MIT fork under the midrender org, reached 0.11.0 in July 2026 and renders headless through Puppeteer, but it is "thinner and slower-moving" and has no official skills ([Meldron09 research doc](https://github.com/Meldron09/Understand-Anything/blob/research/programmatic-video/docs/research/programmatic-video.md); [Meldron09 issue #3](https://github.com/Meldron09/Understand-Anything/issues/3)). For an agent, less public example code means more errors.

## GSAP decides what moves inside each frame

The hub decides how frames are captured. The animation library decides what the frames contain, and here one fact changes the calculus: **GSAP became 100% free on 30 April 2025, including every former Club plugin, "even for commercial use"** ([CSS-Tricks](https://css-tricks.com/gsap-is-now-completely-free-even-for-commercial-use/); [GreenSock on X](https://x.com/greensock/status/1917618886796574918)). The gsap@3.15.0 npm tarball contains SplitText, DrawSVG, MorphSVG, MotionPath, Flip, ScrambleText, CustomEase, CustomWiggle and PixiPlugin ([npm gsap](https://registry.npmjs.org/gsap)). Those plugins map almost one-to-one onto a copy lesson. SplitText handles word-by-word kinetic type, DrawSVG draws underlines and circles around key phrases, MorphSVG turns a weak headline's shape into a strong one, and ScrambleText gives a decoding reveal for a rewritten line. The one licence condition bars using GSAP in no-code visual animation builders that compete with Webflow ([GSAP Standard License](https://gsap.com/community/standard-license/)). Generating MP4s from code is far from that, so it does not bite. GSAP is also what HyperFrames drives internally, using "a paused GSAP timeline registered on window.__timelines, seeked frame by frame" ([themenonlab](https://themenonlab.blog/blog/hyperframes-claude-code-writes-renders-videos)). Moving from hand-written smoothstep easing in `render(t)` to a paused GSAP master timeline seeked to `t` keeps your determinism and gives you a far richer motion vocabulary. Lines can also be placed on labels taken from narration timestamps.

The permissive alternatives are **anime.js 4.5.0** (MIT, zero dependencies, timeline system) and **Motion 14.9.0** (MIT, formerly Framer Motion, usable from vanilla JS) ([npm registry](https://registry.npmjs.org/motion); [licenses.dev animejs](https://licenses.dev/npm/animejs/4.5.0)). Plain CSS animations, transitions, SVG/SMIL and the Web Animations API are separate clocks that do not follow `requestAnimationFrame` or `Date` overrides ([timeweb](https://github.com/tungs/timeweb)). They can still be made seekable by pausing every entry in `document.getAnimations()` and setting `currentTime`. Popmotion and Velocity are superseded or unmaintained (velocity-animate's last release was 2018), and Mo.js moves slowly ([npm registry](https://registry.npmjs.org/velocity-animate); [awesome-web-animation](https://github.com/sergey-pimenov/awesome-web-animation)).

Canvas and vector libraries add looks that DOM elements cannot easily produce. All of them become deterministic the same way: stop the library's own ticker and draw from an explicit `t`, or let GSAP tween their properties.

Versions and licences above come from the npm registry ([npm pixi.js](https://registry.npmjs.org/pixi.js); [npm p5](https://registry.npmjs.org/p5); [npm @tsparticles/engine](https://registry.npmjs.org/@tsparticles/engine)).

**Lottie** is the right format for reusable icons and reaction loops, less so for bespoke choreography. The actively maintained runtime is dotlottie-web 0.82.0 (MIT, ThorVG compiled to WebAssembly), and its `setFrame(n)` "renders the animation at the specified frame regardless of its current state" ([LottieFiles blog](https://lottiefiles.com/blog/working-with-lottie-animations/new-dotlottie-web-player)). lottie-web's last release was May 2025. An agent can author Lottie through python-lottie's typed object model ([PyPI lottie](https://pypi.org/project/lottie/)) or through the official Lottie Creator MCP (`npx -y @lottiefiles/creator-mcp@latest`) ([LottieFiles blog](https://lottiefiles.com/blog/working-with-lottie-animations/your-ai-can-now-control-lottie-creator-heres-what-that-actually-means)). Writing raw Lottie JSON directly is unreliable: the OmniLottie paper (CVPR 2026) notes that prior LLM approaches emitting raw JSON have low success rates because of the strict format ([arXiv 2603.02138](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.02138)). Both HyperFrames and Remotion (`@remotion/lottie`) seek Lottie per frame, so a small branded icon library in crimson and aubergine is a cheap way to build series consistency.

**Rive** is built for interactive state machines, which a linear video does not need. Its web runtimes are MIT and very active (2.49.0, 29 October 2026), and it added Luau scripting in January 2026 ([Rive blog](https://rive.app/blog/scripting-is-live-in-rive)). But .riv files are authored in the Rive editor. The official Rive MCP is served by the running desktop app ([mcpservers.org Rive](https://mcpservers.org/servers/rive-mcp)). An unofficial, source-available rive-mcp can write .riv files from JSON and render them headless, but it has 25 stars and forbids modification ([ODU33104/rive-mcp](https://github.com/ODU33104/rive-mcp)). Use Rive only if an artist hands you a rigged character. **Theatre.js** stalled on npm at 0.7.2 in May 2024, and its studio editor is AGPL ([npm @theatre/core](https://registry.npmjs.org/@theatre/core); [theatre-js](https://github.com/theatre-js/theatre)), so avoid it for new work.

Determinism has a short list of traps that apply to every library above: unseeded `Math.random`, web fonts arriving after the first frame (await `document.fonts.ready`), image and video decode timing, CSS transitions triggered by class changes, and Lottie files whose own frame rate does not match the output's. HyperFrames' seekable adapters take care of the clock side of these. A DIY harness must handle all of them by hand.

## Python, shader and ffmpeg renderers serve specialist roles

**Manim Community** (MIT) is the strongest non-browser renderer and is very active. v0.22.1 shipped on 27 October 2026, v0.21.0 added Typst text "without requiring a TeX installation" plus a 2.2x Cairo speed-up, and since v0.19.0 it writes video through PyAV with "only pip" needed ([Manim releases](https://github.com/ManimCommunity/manim/releases); [r/manim release thread](https://lr.us.psf.lt/r/manim/comments/1i5s5eb/manim_community_v0190_has_been_released)). Claude writes a Python `Scene` and runs `manim render`. It excels at graphs, geometry and diagrams, but reads as a maths look by default and has no built-in voiceover timeline, so audio and captions are muxed afterwards. For copywriting lessons its role is narrow: a conversion-rate chart or a funnel diagram rendered as a clip and composited in. One research team names it the fallback "for math/science concepts" ([Meldron09 issue #3](https://github.com/Meldron09/Understand-Anything/issues/3)).

**Fragment shaders** are the cheapest route to rich animated backgrounds: noise, liquid gradients, grain, halftone dots. glslViewer can render headless straight to video ([devloop01 gist](https://Gist.github.com/devloop01)), and ghee pins shader time to frame × timestep for reproducible output piped to ffmpeg ([PyPI ghee-render](https://pypi.org/project/ghee-render/0.2.0/)). In practice the simplest route is a full-screen shader inside the same headless-Chromium page, with `uTime = frame / fps`. Shadertoy code should be treated as non-commercial unless its author says otherwise, so have Claude write its own noise and grain shaders.

**ffmpeg** is always the last step. It handles mixing and loudness, and it can also produce a whole simple lesson by itself through `drawtext`, ASS karaoke captions, `zoompan` push-ins and `xfade` transitions. Writing complex motion directly in filter syntax is brittle for an agent, though, so use it for assembly, caption burn-in and a contact-sheet QA pass, not for choreography. **Editly** (stale since May 2025, latest tag a release candidate) and **MoviePy** (compositing only, no animation model) add little over ffmpeg plus a browser ([Meldron09 research doc](https://github.com/Meldron09/Understand-Anything/blob/research/programmatic-video/docs/research/programmatic-video.md)). Cloud JSON render APIs such as Shotstack, Creatomate and JSON2Video run about **$0.10–0.40 per rendered minute** ([motionbox](https://motionbox.io/blog/best-json2video-alternatives-in-2026); [wireflow JSON2Video pricing](https://www.wireflow.ai/blog/json2video-pricing)). They suit high-volume templated output, but their JSON templates limit bespoke motion design and add a recurring cost you do not need.

## 3D runs on CPU, but cost per frame varies eightyfold

The research team benchmarked 3D directly in a 4-vCPU, no-GPU Linux container on 31 October 2026, at 1080x1920. Those local measurements, not published sources, are the basis for the numbers below. The most important finding is that **the GL backend matters more than the engine**. A 42-mesh three.js scene with `MeshToonMaterial`, a 3-step gradient map and `OutlineEffect` rendered at **180 ms per frame on Chromium's default SwiftShader, but 66 ms when ANGLE was pointed at Mesa llvmpipe** (`--use-angle=gl-egl --use-gl=angle`). That backend only works after installing `libegl1 libegl-mesa0 libgl1-mesa-dri libgbm1`. An independent benchmark found the same thing: about 24 s per 3D page on Chrome's default software path versus about 6 s with ANGLE on llvmpipe, and it warns that `--disable-gpu` silently forces SwiftShader back on ([microlink.io](https://microlink.io/blog/webgl-without-a-gpu)). Remotion exposes this as a `gl` option (`swiftshader`, `swangle`, `angle-egl`) ([Remotion docs](https://remotion.dev/docs/render-frames)). Full-screen post-processing is the hidden cost. The stock three.js `HalftonePass` took **762 ms per frame on llvmpipe and 4.2 s on SwiftShader**, which would make a minute of halftone video take over two hours. The fix is to write grain, halftone and colour offset as one fused custom shader rather than a chain of stock passes.

| 3D route (local test, 4 vCPU, 1080x1920) | Per frame | 60 s at 30 fps | Styles | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| three.js toon + outlines, ANGLE→llvmpipe | ~60–70 ms | ~2 min | Cel or toon 3D, low-poly, 3D type, shader backgrounds | **Very good** for accents and even whole scenes |
| three.js toon, SwiftShader (default) | ~140–180 ms | ~5 min | Same | Good; set the flags instead |
| three.js + stock HalftonePass | 0.8–4.2 s | 25 min to 2 h+ | Spider-Verse dots | Only with a fused custom shader |
| Blender Workbench | ~0.9 s | ~27 min | Flat, matcap, "designed clay" with outlines | Previews; some clean looks |
| Blender EEVEE, 1 sample (+Freestyle) | ~2 s | ~1 h | Toon, NPR, Line Art, claymation, stop-motion | Premium episodes |
| Blender EEVEE, 4 samples | ~5.4 s | ~2.7 h | Smoother toon and NPR | Hero shots |
| Blender Cycles CPU, 16 samples + denoise | ~24–47 s | 12–24 h | Photoreal | Poor |

**three.js** (and React Three Fiber, or `@remotion/three` inside Remotion) therefore suits this series well, provided the look commits to toon. Your toon tests were the right instinct, and the brand gives a concrete recipe. A 3-step `gradientMap` built from an aubergine shadow, a crimson mid-tone and a pale highlight turns any mesh into on-brand cel shading at near-zero cost. The `OutlineEffect` add-on gives permanent toon outlines, but only on objects using `MeshToonMaterial` ([threejs.org](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/OutlineEffect.html)), and forum advice reserves `OutlinePass` for interaction highlights ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/rendering-style/12593.md)). Babylon.js and PlayCanvas work in the same harness, but three.js has far more public example code for Claude to draw on. Gaussian splats (Spark, MIT, needs three r180+) can be mixed into three.js scenes ([sparkjs.dev](https://sparkjs.dev/)), but they are photoreal captures that clash with an illustrated brand.

**Blender via `bpy`** is fully scriptable from Claude Code. Install the `bpy` pip wheel (5.2.3 LTS for Python 3.13 in the test) plus the Mesa EGL libraries, write a Python scene script, render PNG frames, and encode with ffmpeg. The local test contradicts an October 2026 render-farm article claiming EEVEE cannot run headless ([iRender](https://irendering.net/eevee-next-headless-dilemma-cloud-render-farm-guide/)). EEVEE ran without a GPU, X server or Xvfb once `libEGL.so.1` was present. Blender 5.0 added Grease Pencil motion blur ([Blender 5.0 notes](https://developer.blender.org/docs/release_notes/5.0/grease_pencil/)). Blender 5.1 (17 March 2026) made scripted Grease Pencil fills simpler ([Blender 5.1 notes](https://developer.blender.org/docs/release_notes/5.1/grease_pencil/)) and introduced EEVEE NPR stylisation integrated with scene shading, according to a single source ([Blender 5.1 release page](https://hn.jakechampion.name/reader/https://www.blender.org/download/releases/5-1)). Blender is the right tool for looks the browser cannot fake: claymation thumbprint textures, 2D-in-3D Grease Pencil, Line Art, painterly strokes and true camera projection. At 1–3 hours per minute on 4 cores, though, it belongs to the occasional hero shot, rendered with alpha and dropped into the HTML composition. Render many frames per process, because the first frame costs 2–15x more while shaders compile. The official Blender Lab MCP server lets Claude drive a *running* Blender 5.1+ session through the Python API, and blender.org warns it "will execute LLM generated code in Blender without any guards" ([StraySpark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/official-blender-mcp-server-comparison-2026)). In a container, plain `bpy` scripts are simpler.

**Recurring characters** are where the routes diverge most. The most agent-friendly, CPU-cheap and licence-clean option is a **2D cut-out puppet**: layered SVG parts with pivots, animated by GSAP, with mouth shapes from **Rhubarb Lip Sync**. Rhubarb is a CLI that takes a WAV and outputs 6 basic mouth shapes (A–F) plus optional G, H and X as JSON, and it is more accurate when given the script via `--dialogFile` ([Rhubarb](https://github.com/DanielSWolf/rhubarb-lip-sync)). Blinks, breathing and head bobs on stressed words are a few lines of seeded code. For a 3D character, the clean sources are CC0. Quaternius's Universal Animation Library offers 130+ animations on one rig ([quaternius.com](https://quaternius.com/packs/universalanimationlibrary.html); [Digital Production](https://digitalproduction.com/2026/02/10/130-animations-one-rig-zero-drama/)), Kenney packs are CC0 ([OpenGameArt](https://opengameart.org/content/blocky-characters)), and MPFB 2 output is reported as CC0 ([CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2025/03/check-out-open-source-blender-character-generation-plugin-mpfb-2)). Several familiar routes are dead ends for automation. Mixamo has no API and is unmaintained ([Cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/mixamo-to-blender-2026)). **Ready Player Me shut down on 31 January 2026** after Netflix acquired it ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/netflix-acquires-avatar-creation-platform-ready-player-me)). AccuRIG is a Windows desktop app ([CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2025/07/rig-and-animate-3d-characters-for-free-with-accurig-2-0/)). Spine needs an editor licence for every product that uses its runtimes ([Spine Editor License](https://en.esotericsoftware.com/spine-editor-license)), and Live2D models must be authored in its GUI ([Live2D](https://www.live2d.com/en/sdk/license/)). AI 3D generators carry very different licence terms. Meshy's free tier is reportedly non-commercial ([Meshy docs](https://docs.meshy.ai/en/webapp/pricing)). Hunyuan3D's licence excludes the EU, UK and South Korea ([Hunyuan3D issue](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hunyuan3D-2/issues/6)), which rules it out if you or your audience are there. TRELLIS.2's default pipeline uses a CC BY-NC background remover ([CGWorld.jp](https://cgworld.jp/flashnews/01-202601-TRELLIS2.html)).

Research also explains why primitive-built 3D characters often look "off". Appeal and eeriness are driven mainly by how stylised the **material** is, not the shape, and abstract shapes with realistic materials rate as especially eerie ([Zell et al., SIGGRAPH Asia 2015](https://cg.cs.tu-dortmund.de/downloads/publications/2015/sigasia2015-slides.pdf); [arXiv 2110.04083](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.04083)). A capsule-and-sphere character under soft EEVEE shadows sits right in that mismatch. The same shapes under a flat 3-tone brand ramp with a bold outline do not. If 3D tests have felt uncanny, the cure is to stylise the shading fully, not to add realism.

## Desktop bridges and generative AI are add-ons, not foundations

Claude Code can reach the big desktop tools through MCP, but almost every bridge needs the GUI app running locally with a panel or add-on installed, so few run in a cloud container. **No official After Effects MCP exists** as of October 2026 ([Vagon](https://vagon.io/blog/how-to-use-claude-code-with-after-effects)). Community servers such as Dakkshin/after-effects-mcp (MIT) work through an ExtendScript bridge panel inside AE ([lobehub](https://lobehub.com/mcp/dakkshin-after-effects-mcp)). Headless AE rendering through nexrender wraps `aerender`, needs Windows or macOS, and relies on a "render-only" mode whose legality for server use is disputed on Adobe's own forums ([nexrender](https://www.GitHub.com/inlife/nexrender); [Adobe community](https://community.adobe.com/questions-529/after-effects-web-server-license-69223)). DaVinci Resolve's external scripting is gated to the paid Studio edition ([samuelgursky/davinci-resolve-mcp](https://github.com/samuelgursky/davinci-resolve-mcp)). Adobe's official "Adobe for Creativity" connector (28 April 2026) offers 50+ tools across Photoshop, Firefly, Express and Premiere ([Adobe blog](https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2026/04/28/adobe-for-creativity-connector)), but works at Express and Firefly level rather than full desktop features ([MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/claude-mcp-adobe-vs-photoshop-premiere-what-it-does)). Figma's official MCP is read-oriented design context, useful for pulling brand tokens, not for animation ([Figma Help Center](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/39888612464151-Claude-Code-and-Figma-Set-up-the-MCP-server)). These bridges only make sense if you already own AE templates. For a solo creator starting fresh they add licence cost, a GUI dependency and the risk of running arbitrary generated scripts against your project files.

Generative AI is useful in two places. Narration and sound are the first, and your offline Kokoro voice already covers narration at zero marginal cost. ElevenLabs is the hosted alternative, with commercial rights from the $6 Starter plan, SFX at $0.12 per minute and music at $0.15 per minute on its API ([ElevenLabs API pricing](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing/api)). Occasional B-roll is the second. OpenAI's Sora 2 API was reportedly shut down on 24 September 2026 ([FrameSurfer](https://framesurfer.com/blogs/sora-2-pricing)), leaving Veo 3.1 at roughly $0.08–0.40 per second depending on tier ([invideo](https://invideo.io/blog/ai-video-model-pricing/)) and Runway's API at $0.05 per second for gen4_turbo and $0.12 for gen4.5 ([Runway API pricing](https://docs.dev.runwayml.com/guides/pricing/)). Three or four 5-second Veo Fast plates would add about $2 to a lesson. Lip-sync and avatar APIs (Sync from $0.02 per second, HeyGen around $1–4 per minute) exist ([Sync docs](https://sync.so/docs/api-reference/api-overview); [G2](https://www.g2.com/articles/heygen-api-pricing)), but they produce realistic faces, which fight an illustrated brand. Generated video also does not hold a character or palette steady across shots without reference-image features, whereas code reproduces crimson and aubergine exactly every time. Keep type, captions and brand layers code-rendered and treat AI footage as an occasional background plate. This matches the 2026 trend framing of AI as a production aid in "hybrid workflows" rather than as the aesthetic itself ([Envato Author Hub](https://author.envato.com/hub/video-motion-design-trends-2026/)).

## Most of the 26 styles are 2D and cheap

Code can produce at least 26 recognisable motion styles, but only some suit a narrated copy lesson, and the 2026 trend data points the same way as the cost data. Canva's 2026 report is themed **"Imperfect by Design"**, and its "Notes App Chic" scrapbook trend comes with collage-element searches **up 90%** ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251210696597/en/Canva-Unveils-2026-Design-Trends-The-Year-of-Imperfect-by-Design); [Campaign Brief](https://campaignbrief.com/canvas-2026-design-trends-report-highlights-shift-toward-imperfect-by-design/)). Searches for "brutalist design" and "type poster" rose **77% year over year** ([Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/design/canvas-2026-trend-predictions-have-filled-me-with-hope)), and Adobe lists collage and tactile texture among its 2026 trends ([Adobe Express](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/design-trends-2026)). Every one of those looks is 2D, templatable and native to HTML.

The kinetic-caption convention most viewers recognise, the Hormozi style, uses a thick all-caps sans (typically Montserrat Extra Bold), white words with a yellow, orange or green active word, and a chunky black outline, revealing one or two words at a time ([ascynd.io](https://ascynd.io/en/blog/hormozi-captions)). For a Crimson & Aubergine brand, my judgment is to translate the convention rather than copy its colours. Use light cream ink on a deep aubergine card, and mark the active or key word with a crimson highlight bar behind it rather than crimson text. Crimson and aubergine are both dark, saturated hues, and crimson text on aubergine will not reach the 4.5:1 contrast your current style rules require. The same logic covers grain and halftone. Crimson dots on an aubergine field read as print, while full-saturation crimson over large areas will tire the eye across 60 seconds.

The evidence base for pacing and captions is thinner than the style trends. TikTok's own ad guidance says to hook in the first 6 seconds, state the proposition in the first 3, and show 5–10 words per second of on-screen text ([TikTok Ads](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/creative-best-practices)). Narration at 150–180 words per minute is 2.5–3 words per second, so word-synced captions sit comfortably inside that ceiling. Meta's Reels-ad safe zone keeps **14% at the top, 35% at the bottom and 6% at the sides** free of text, roughly 269 px, 672 px and 65 px at 1080x1920 ([Billo](https://billo.app/blog/meta-ads-safe-zones)). TikTok publishes no official zone, and third-party measurements of its right rail and description area disagree ([Uplads](https://uplads.com/tools/safe-zone-checker); [daohuy.com](https://daohuy.com/en/blog/post-vertical-subtitle-safe-zones-9-16/)). The most rigorous evidence comes from learning science. The **coherence principle** (cut extraneous material) held in 23 of 23 experiments with a median d = 0.86, and **signalling** held in 24 of 28 with d = 0.41. The **redundancy principle** warns that narration plus graphics beats narration plus graphics plus duplicated on-screen text ([Mayer & Fiorella](https://resolve-he.cambridge.org/core/books/cambridge-handbook-of-multimedia-learning/principles-for-reducing-extraneous-processing-in-multimedia-learning-coherence-signaling-redundancy-spatial-contiguity-and-temporal-contiguity-principles/CD5B7AE1279A9AB81F8EEBB53DBEC86E)). That creates a real tension with verbatim word-by-word captions, which sound-off feeds all but require. The defensible compromise is short caption pages (2–4 words) with one signalled keyword, while the example copy itself stays the dominant visual. Decorative hype that fails the coherence test should go. No study found here shows that kinetic captions specifically improve retention, and the widely quoted "85% watched muted" statistic has no primary source.

## The recommended stack

The stack below keeps what already works, your HTML stage, Playwright, ffmpeg and offline narration, and upgrades the three weak points: hand-tuned easing, estimated caption timing, and having no route to 3D other than slow Blender renders.

| Layer | Recommendation | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Composition hub** | **HyperFrames** as default; keep your own Playwright `render(t)` harness as a fallback; Remotion if you prefer React and stay at 3 people or fewer | Same seek-per-frame model you already use, Apache-2.0, official Claude Code skills, built-in audio mixing and captions block |
| **Motion** | One paused **GSAP** master timeline per scene: SplitText, DrawSVG, MorphSVG, Flip, CustomEase | Free for commercial use; covers kinetic type, annotation and morphs; seekable |
| **Narration timing** | Keep Kokoro TTS; add **whisper.cpp** word timestamps (or Remotion's `createTikTokStyleCaptions`) instead of length-weighted estimates | Caption words land on the spoken frame; also drives SFX and emphasis beats |
| **Illustration** | Hand-coded SVG scenes; Rough.js for hand-drawn accents; a small dotLottie icon set in brand colours | Inspectable DOM, crisp text, cheap, consistent across episodes |
| **Texture** | One fused grain or halftone shader, or SVG `feTurbulence` | Hits the 2026 tactile trend; avoids the 0.8–4 s per frame cost of stock passes |
| **Character** | 2D cut-out SVG puppet plus **Rhubarb** mouth shapes, procedural blinks and breathing | Cheapest consistent mascot; licence-clean |
| **3D accents** | three.js toon (brand 3-step gradient map plus outlines) in the same page, Chromium launched with `--use-angle=gl-egl --use-gl=angle` and Mesa EGL installed | ~60–70 ms per frame; title cards, 3D type, a toon object cameo |
| **Premium shots** | Blender `bpy` EEVEE at 1–4 samples, Grease Pencil or Line Art, rendered with alpha and composited | Claymation and NPR looks the browser cannot fake; 1–3 h per minute, so seconds not minutes |
| **Specialist inserts** | Manim for an occasional chart; Veo or Runway plates only as background B-roll | Narrow, cheap, optional |
| **Final assembly and QA** | ffmpeg mix, `loudnorm` to your -14 LUFS target; contact sheets with ffmpeg `tile`; text bounding boxes checked against a ~270 px top and ~670 px bottom safe zone | The agent cannot watch the video, so it must inspect sampled frames and geometry |

Avoid Motion Canvas (dormant), Theatre.js (stalled), timecut and timesnap (stale, miss CSS animation), Blender Cycles for anything longer than a still, Mixamo or Ready Player Me in an automated character pipeline, copied Shadertoy code, and After Effects or Resolve bridges unless you already own templates in them.

## Conclusion

The real constraint on agent-made motion graphics is no longer rendering. Every option above produces a correct 1080x1920 MP4 headless. The constraint is motion *quality* that an agent cannot see. Claude can only judge stills, so the winning tools are the ones that make every moment inspectable on demand: seekable timelines, DOM geometry that can be measured against safe zones, and deterministic frames that can be sampled into contact sheets. That favours SVG and HTML over canvas, GSAP over hand-rolled easing, and code-built characters over generated ones. It also explains why HeyGen and Remotion both converged on the browser as their engine, and why Anthropic's Claude Motion likewise builds animations as code rather than with a video model.

Two implications follow for this series. First, the HyperFrames-or-Remotion decision turns on licence and authoring language, not capability. With a free Apache-2.0 option that matches your existing architecture, Remotion's advantage narrows to its caption tooling and tutorial volume. Second, 3D's place in a Crimson & Aubergine lesson depends more on stylisation than on engine choice. A toon ramp built from the brand colours makes cheap browser 3D look designed, while realistic shading on simple shapes looks uncanny however long it renders. The open questions worth testing next are HyperFrames' 9:16 caption behaviour in practice, and whether a 2D cut-out mascot with Rhubarb lip sync reads as warmly as your faceless illustrated narrator.
