## Q1. Style catalogue: definition, techniques, examples, code build, difficulty, suitability

### Takeaway
For a code-driven pipeline (Claude Code writing HTML/CSS/SVG/Canvas, Remotion, Blender Python, ffmpeg) making narrated 60–90s copywriting lessons, the best core styles are **kinetic typography with word-level captions**, **paper-collage / Vox-style mixed media**, **flat/editorial illustration with SVG shape morphs**, **bento/neo-brutalist UI cards**, and **data-viz builds**. All are 2D, deterministic and easy to template. Add **grain/riso textures** and **hype-edit punch-ins** as finishing layers. Leave 3D, claymation, Spider-Verse and fluid sims as accent shots, since they cost far more in a code pipeline.

### Cited Findings
- **Vox-style collage technique**: "Old photographs sit on warm, textured paper backgrounds like physical objects — drop shadows, torn edges, a slow push-in with subtle 2.5D parallax between layers." The stutter comes from animating "on twos" (each pose held 2 frames, so 12 drawings/s at 24fps). Tricks include Posterize Time at 12fps. Traditional toolkit: Premiere Pro, After Effects, GEOlayers/Mapbox for maps, Photoshop for cutouts, and texture libraries for grain and paper. Note this is a tutorial imitating Vox, not an official Vox source. — [easy-peasy.ai: How to make Vox-style videos](https://easy-peasy.ai/blog/how-to-make-vox-style-videos-with-ai)
- **Open-source agent pipelines already exist for this look.** "Vox Director" is an agent skill that automates Vox-style videos end to end on Atlas Cloud plus ffmpeg, where "each beat is a finished collage poster made by a text-to-image model". There is also a "VOX-COLLAGE-BROLL" SKILL.md and a MiniMax "paper-collage-explainer-generator" skill. — [GitHub Alisa0808/vox-director](https://github.com/Alisa0808/vox-director); [VOX-COLLAGE-BROLL SKILL.md](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/MegaTroll222/VOX-COLLAGE-BROLL@main/SKILL.md); [skills.sh paper-collage-explainer-generator](https://skills.sh/minimax-ai/minimax-h3/paper-collage-explainer-generator)
- Freelancers list After Effects, Illustrator, Premiere Pro, Canva and CapCut as the tools for collage explainers. — [Contra listing](https://contra.com/p/mAYbEXPk-creative-explainer-videos-with-collage)
- **Hormozi-style captions (kinetic word-by-word)**: "A thick, bold, all-caps sans-serif font, most commonly Montserrat Extra Bold or a close alternative called The Bold Font". White for normal words, yellow or orange for the highlighted word (green #39FF14 is an alternative). The captions reveal "one or two words at a time in sync with speech, with the active word highlighted", and every word gets a chunky black outline for legibility. — [ascynd.io Hormozi captions](https://ascynd.io/en/blog/hormozi-captions); [autocaptionsstudio guide](https://autocaptionsstudio.com/guides/hormozi-style-captions.html); [blitzreels](https://blitzreels.com/captions/hormozi-style); [Kapwing template](https://www.kapwing.com/explore/hormozi-style-captions-template)
- **Spider-Verse / halftone in code**: Three.js Journey has a "Halftone Shading" lesson that applies halftone "like in Spider‑Man: Into the Spider‑Verse", layered on top of subtle light shading. Three.js also ships a post-processing `HalftoneShader.js` ("RGB Halftone shader", shapes: dot, ellipse, line, square). — [Three.js Journey halftone lesson](https://threejs-journey.com/lessons/halftone-shading-shaders); [HalftoneShader.js source](https://sources.debian.org/src/three.js/111%2Bdfsg1-3/examples/jsm/shaders/HalftoneShader.js)
- **Spider-Verse look in Blender** (node-editor and EEVEE recipes) is well documented. — [GarageFarm: Recreating the Spider-Verse look in the Blender node editor](https://blog.garagefarm.net/blog/recreating-the-spider-verse-look-in-the-blender-node-editor); [BlenderNation 7 steps Spiderverse shader](https://www.blendernation.com/2024/01/15/7-steps-to-make-a-spiderverse-shader-in-blender/); [BlenderNation EEVEE Spider-Verse shader](https://www.blendernation.com/2019/02/18/spider-verse-shader-in-blender2-8-eevee/)
- **Remotion caption implementation**: no fetched source covered this, because remotion.dev was blocked. A search summary noted that a Remotion build needs word-level timestamps (from Whisper or Deepgram) and a per-word component that switches the active word's colour on the matching frame. — [search synthesis; see Kapwing/ascynd sources above for the style itself]

**Tool-fit summary (inference):**
- **Remotion (React)**: best overall hub for styles 1, 2, 3, 8, 10, 11, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19, 20, 22, 25 and 26. It is frame-deterministic, takes VO timestamps directly, and renders 1080×1920 MP4.
- **Raw HTML/CSS/SVG + headless capture** (Playwright frame-stepping, or a library like `timecut`/`webvideo`): fine for simple styles, but non-deterministic timing makes frame-accurate capture harder than Remotion.
- **Canvas/WebGL/GLSL** (inside Remotion via `@remotion/three` or a `<canvas>` keyed to `useCurrentFrame`): use for halftone, grain, glitch, liquid and depth-displacement shaders (5, 6, 7, 13, 14).
- **Blender (Python `bpy`, CLI render)**: use for claymation, true 3D type, camera projection and Spider-Verse 3D (7, 9, 12, 13, 21). It is slow to render, so treat it as an accent-shot generator and drop its output into Remotion as video.
- **ffmpeg**: assembly, loudness normalisation (`loudnorm`), grain/noise passes, datamosh, ASS karaoke captions, `zoompan`/`xfade` for a no-JS fallback. It is always the last step.
- **Manim**: an option for maths- or diagram-heavy data viz.

## Q2. Which styles are trending in 2025–2026 for educational and B2B/LinkedIn content?

### Takeaway
The 2026 trend signal across Canva, Adobe and Envato is **"imperfect, human, tactile"**: collage, scrapbook/notes-app doodles, grain and paper, oversized and brutalist type, and hybrid AI-plus-human workflows. Polished flat corporate vectors are fading. LinkedIn has invested in a vertical video feed, which makes 9:16 explainers viable for B2B.

### Inferences
- For a copywriting-lesson series aimed at both creators (TikTok/Reels) and B2B (LinkedIn), the strongest on-trend, code-friendly combination is: **kinetic type captions + notes-app/collage mixed media + paper grain + bento/brutalist summary cards**. This hits "Imperfect by Design", "Notes App Chic" and the brutalist-type search rise while staying templatable.
- Flat corporate "Alegria" explainers look dated against the 2026 imperfection trend. If used, add texture and hand-drawn annotation.
- AI footage is trending as a *production aid* (hybrid workflow), not as the aesthetic itself. Keep typography and brand layers code-rendered.

## Q3. Evidence and benchmarks: pacing, captions, hooks, safe zones, loudness

### Takeaway
Hard evidence is thin. TikTok's own ad guidance (hook in the first 6s, core message in the first 3s, 5–10 words/sec of on-screen text) and Meta's Reels-ad safe-zone percentages (14% top / 35% bottom / 6% sides) are the most authoritative numbers. Pacing ("change visuals every 1.5–2s") and loudness (-14 LUFS) are community conventions, not platform specs. Learning-science research (Mayer) supports signalling and coherence, but warns that on-screen text duplicating narration can hurt learning. That is a real tension with full word-by-word captions.

### Cited Findings

**Hooks and early seconds**
- TikTok performance-ads guidance: "prioritize the hook in the first 6 seconds to boost engagement and increase watch time", "introduce the content proposition in the first 3 seconds for better recall and awareness", and "display 5-10 words per second when using text". Captions or text overlays are recommended to give context. — [TikTok Ads: Creative best practices for performance ads](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/creative-best-practices) (quoted via search snippet; page not fetchable in this session)
- The frequently quoted "63% of top-CTR TikToks deliver the key message in the first 3 seconds" could not be sourced in this session. — [no source found]
- An academic paper (ResearchGate, partially garbled) reports average view durations of 11.8s for YouTube Shorts and 9.1s for Facebook Reels, with drop-off around 8s and 4s respectively, and calls the first three seconds critical. — [ResearchGate: Impact of Short Form Content on Consumer Attention and Marketing ROI](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/390814911_The_Impact_of_Short_Form_Content_on_Consumer_Attention_and_Marketing_ROI)
- The widely repeated "65% drop off in 3 seconds" stat appears without a primary source. — [Medium: techwithlandon](https://medium.com/@techwithlandon/want-over-70-retention-on-your-short-form-videos-heres-how-to-do-it-5ce7bae94a43) (unsourced)

**Pacing**
- No peer-reviewed study of shot length or cuts per second on TikTok was found. A 2026 creator playbook recommends changing visuals every 1.5–2 seconds. — [Aibrify: YouTube Shorts retention curve playbook (2026)](https://aibrify.com/blog/youtube-shorts-retention-curve-playbook); [Shortzly retention strategies](https://shortzly.com/blog/short-form-video-retention-strategies) (blogs, no study cited)

**Length**
- Fanpage Karma found the highest TikTok interaction rates for videos of 30–90s. — [Fanpage Karma](https://www.fanpagekarma.com/insights/optimal-tiktok-video-length/)
- Socialinsider's 2026 brand benchmark found videos under 30s had the highest engagement rate, with a dip at 30–60s. — [Socialinsider video statistics](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/social-media-video-statistics). These two sources conflict.
- A 2026 arXiv longitudinal study of finfluencer TikToks found only a negligible association between duration and engagement. — [arXiv 2607.05203](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.05203)
- A conference paper reports a positive linear effect of length on TikTok engagement (Cheng & Li 2024), with a platform average of 42.68s (Statista 2024). — [Marketing Trends Congress 2025 paper](https://archives.marketing-trends-congress.com/2025/pages/PDF/060.pdf)
- LinkedIn vertical guidance from third parties: keep it under 60s, with 30–90s performing best (unverified). Max length figures conflict across sources. — [Exemplary.ai](https://exemplary.ai/blog/linkedin-vertical-video-specs); [Sendspark](https://blog.sendspark.com/linkedin-video-specs)

**Captions and sound-off**
- The claim that 75–85% of social video is watched muted, and "~85% of LinkedIn video watched on mute", has no primary source found. — [search synthesis of LinkedIn spec blogs, e.g. LigoSocial](https://ligosocial.com/blog/linkedin-video-post-best-practices) (unverified)
- One claim says hard-coded first-frame captions lift sound-off retention 25–40%, but no study is cited. — [Shortzly](https://shortzly.com/blog/short-form-video-retention-strategies) (unverified)
- LinkedIn reportedly offers auto-captions, but third parties recommend burned-in captions or uploaded SRT files for reliability. — [LinkedIn spec guides via search](https://quso.ai/blog/linkedin-video-specs)
- The often-cited "captions increase view time by 12% (Facebook)" claim could not be sourced. — [no source found]

**Learning science (most rigorous evidence)**
- **Coherence principle** (remove extraneous material): supported in 23 of 23 experiments, median effect size d = 0.86. **Signalling principle** (cues that highlight structure): supported in 24 of 28 experiments, median d = 0.41. **Redundancy principle**: people learn better from graphics plus narration than from graphics, narration *and* on-screen text. **Temporal contiguity**: present corresponding animation and narration simultaneously. — [Mayer & Fiorella, Cambridge Handbook of Multimedia Learning: Principles for reducing extraneous processing](https://resolve-he.cambridge.org/core/books/cambridge-handbook-of-multimedia-learning/principles-for-reducing-extraneous-processing-in-multimedia-learning-coherence-signaling-redundancy-spatial-contiguity-and-temporal-contiguity-principles/CD5B7AE1279A9AB81F8EEBB53DBEC86E)
- A meta-analysis of 92 articles and 591 effects found the largest effects for removing seductive detail, modality, personalization, the multimedia principle and sentence-level coherence. Principles have boundary conditions, for example being stronger for low-knowledge learners. — [search result summary; NSF PAR listing](https://par.nsf.gov/search/award_ids:1661231)

**Safe zones (1080×1920)**
- Meta Ads Guide for Reels ads: keep ~14% of the top, ~35% of the bottom and ~6% on each side free of text and logos. That is ~269px top, ~672px bottom and ~65px sides at 1080×1920. Some guides give a 20–35% bottom range because captions expand. One source notes Meta publishes margins for Reels *ads* only, with no equivalent for organic Reels. — [Billo: Meta ads safe zones 2026](https://billo.app/blog/meta-ads-safe-zones); [AdNabu](https://blog.adnabu.com/meta-ads/meta-safe-zones/); [House of Marketers](https://houseofmarketers.com/guide-to-safe-zones-tiktok-facebook-instagram-stories-reels/)
- TikTok has no official pixel safe zone. Third-party measurements conflict: about 130px top / 484px bottom / 140px right — [Uplads safe-zone checker](https://uplads.com/tools/safe-zone-checker); about 150px top / 340px bottom — [Reelsafezone](https://reelsafezone.com/); a 180px top band / 380px bottom band — [FileFlippers](https://fileflippers.com/tools/safe-zones). One guide measured TikTok's description at ~340px and recommends a caption baseline ~350px above the bottom edge, noting that app updates shift these values by 20–40px. — [daohuy.com 9:16 caption safe zones](https://daohuy.com/en/blog/post-vertical-subtitle-safe-zones-9-16/)
- LinkedIn: 9:16 at 1080×1920 or 4:5 at 1080×1350, MP4. — [Exemplary.ai](https://exemplary.ai/blog/linkedin-vertical-video-specs). No LinkedIn safe-zone spec was found.

**Loudness**
- No official TikTok or Instagram loudness spec was found. The most common community target is -14 LUFS integrated with a -1 dBTP true-peak ceiling — [DEV: Mastering for Reels/TikTok/Shorts](https://dev.to/arthurvalle1_a2586dd2b4bc/mastering-for-reels-tiktok-and-youtube-shorts-the-lufs-14-standard-2ck5); [OpenClip LUFS targets](https://openclip.app/learn/audio-normalization). One tool page frames a -16 LUFS target instead — [-16 LUFS page](https://d1lex0gkhzvix9.cloudfront.net/tiktok-instagram-reels-loudness). OpenClip states TikTok has "no official standard".

### Inferences
- **Production defaults** for the series: 1080×1920, 30fps (or 24fps for collage/on-twos looks). Keep critical text inside roughly **y = 270–1250px and x = 65–940px**. That is the intersection of Meta's percentages and TikTok's right-rail (~140px) measurements, and it is conservative enough for LinkedIn. Mix VO to about -14 LUFS / -1 dBTP with ffmpeg `loudnorm=I=-14:TP=-1:LRA=11`, with music ducked roughly 15–20 dB under VO.
- **Hook (0–3s)**: put the lesson's promise as giant kinetic text in frame 1, with no logo intro. Use a punch-in plus SFX at 0s and a visual change by about 1.5s. TikTok's own guidance favours the proposition within 3s.
- **Pacing**: a visual change every ~1.5–3s, often just a caption page turn, an underline draw or a card swap, rather than hard cuts every 0.5s. For teaching, Mayer's coherence result favours fewer, more relevant visuals over constant decorative hype.
- **Caption tension**: full verbatim word-by-word captions conflict with the redundancy principle for learning, but are near-mandatory for sound-off feeds. One compromise is to caption verbatim at 2–4 words per page. Then use *signalling* (colour highlight on the one key word, or show the example headline as the dominant visual) so text is structured rather than decorative. This compromise is inferred and has not been tested.
- **TikTok's 5–10 words/sec text-density ceiling** is higher than typical VO pace (~2.5–3 words/sec at 150–180 wpm). Word-synced captions therefore naturally sit within guidance.

### Gaps
- No primary study on cuts-per-second vs retention for short-form educational video.
- No official TikTok, Instagram or LinkedIn loudness spec.
- No official TikTok or LinkedIn safe-zone pixel spec. Instagram organic Reels margins are also unofficial.
- The official TikTok creative-best-practices page and the Envato trends page could not be fetched (network allowlist). Quotes rely on search snippets.
- I found no rigorous evidence that animated or kinetic captions specifically improve retention or learning compared with static captions.

## Q4. Best code tool per style (summary)

### Takeaway
Use **Remotion as the hub** (React, frame-deterministic, timestamps → animation) for ~80% of the styles. Use **GLSL/Three.js inside Remotion** for texture/shader looks, **Blender `bpy`** for the few true-3D or claymation accent shots, and **ffmpeg** for assembly, captions fallback, datamosh and loudness.

### Cited Findings
- The three.js halftone shader and the Three.js Journey Spider-Verse-style halftone lesson make halftone/comic looks reachable in WebGL. — [Three.js Journey](https://threejs-journey.com/lessons/halftone-shading-shaders); [HalftoneShader.js](https://sources.debian.org/src/three.js/111%2Bdfsg1-3/examples/jsm/shaders/HalftoneShader.js)
- Blender node-editor recipes exist for the Spider-Verse look. — [GarageFarm](https://blog.garagefarm.net/blog/recreating-the-spider-verse-look-in-the-blender-node-editor)
- Collage/Vox look automation has precedent as agent skills using text-to-image plus ffmpeg. — [Vox Director](https://github.com/Alisa0808/vox-director)
